# Project Atlas — Ticketing platform

Nóra Varga leads Project Atlas, the internal support ticketing platform, with a target go-live of 2026-11-15. The approved scope replaces legacy ticket routing with a central API and a service catalogue. The programme code is IT-41. The rollout covers Budapest office requests and the distribution centre's administrative support queue.

The first release accepts equipment faults, access requests and facilities incidents. Payroll changes continue through the HR workflow. The service catalogue must show who accepts a request and which information the requester needs to provide. Teams may keep specialised forms when the central form cannot capture a required attachment without losing its original format.

During acceptance, the support team will replay a sample of closed requests and inspect their routing history. A request must retain its original submission time when reassigned. A search by employee number should return the current support ticket without exposing unrelated confidential attachments. The acceptance group will also check notification delivery from the shared mailbox.

The deployment window begins at 19:00 on the Friday before launch. The duty engineer keeps the previous routing configuration available until Monday's reconciliation is complete. Support will continue accepting urgent telephone requests during the change. A rollback restores the old entry point and leaves any newly accepted ticket in the audit queue for manual reconciliation.

Training is scheduled in the smaller Budapest meeting room. Department coordinators nominate one substitute so that an absence does not delay the launch. The team has reserved two extra morning support shifts for the first week. Minor changes to catalogue wording may be approved during that week; changes to routing rules require a reviewed deployment request.

The service desk supervisor owns queue reconciliation during the launch period. At each shift change, the outgoing coordinator lists requests awaiting reassignment and checks that urgent telephone calls have a ticket number. Duplicate notifications are investigated before a request is closed. A request accepted through the old mailbox remains the responsibility of its assigned team until the receiving queue acknowledges it.

The main operational risk is a routing change that sends confidential requests to a broad support group. Access checks therefore cover both the ticket summary and its attachments. The duty engineer can suspend a faulty catalogue entry while other services continue. Extra supplier support hours require approval against the launch allowance; the project will not treat routine wording changes as chargeable emergency work.
