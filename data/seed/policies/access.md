# Access policy — Temporary accounts

IT approves the Temporary accounts policy. Temporary third-party accounts have a maximum initial validity of 30 days. Renewal requests must arrive 5 days before expiry. Each request identifies a named individual, the sponsoring employee, the required service and the last day of work. A supplier's shared mailbox cannot be used as the account holder.

The service owner checks the requested permissions before the account is enabled. A visitor who only attends a meeting receives a visitor badge, not application access. Warehouse terminal training uses individually assigned practice accounts. The training supervisor must record which station was used so that support can distinguish a training problem from a production issue.

Renewal is a fresh approval, rather than an automatic extension. IT checks the sponsor's confirmation and removes privileges that are no longer needed. If the sponsor has left the company, the department head must nominate another owner. An expired account remains disabled while a late renewal request is reviewed, even when the supplier has an outstanding invoice.

Confidential project folders require an additional approval from the document owner. Access reviews examine both individual permissions and inherited group membership. A successful login alone does not prove that a person needs access to every folder within a service. Emergency access is recorded in the duty log and reviewed on the next working day.

At the end of an engagement, the sponsor confirms that local files and temporary exports have been handed over or removed. IT closes the account and records the closure date. Physical badges and warehouse keys follow the site handover process. The monthly access review checks overdue closures separately from routine password resets.
