# Supplier management policy

Procurement approves the Supplier management policy. Supplier risk is reviewed once per year and service performance is reviewed quarterly. Each agreement must name an internal contract owner and record either a renewal date or a fixed expiry date. The two date types are kept distinct in the purchase register.

A purchase order describes the contracted service, the delivery location and the agreed acceptance conditions. Hardware delivery, monitoring and identity verification can belong to different service lines within the same programme. The contract owner confirms which lines an invoice covers before Finance posts the charge. An approved invoice does not automatically approve an increase in scope.

The annual risk review checks continuity arrangements, subcontractor changes and unresolved security questions. A supplier with no incidents may still need a review when its ownership or hosting arrangement changes. Procurement keeps the review record with the agreement and assigns a deadline for any missing evidence. An expiring certificate is followed up before the next renewal decision.

Quarterly service reviews compare delivery records, support availability and outstanding corrective actions. Project teams attend when an unresolved issue affects their rollout. A service incident is discussed with each relevant provider, but the presence of a provider on a project is not a finding that it caused the incident. Root-cause approval remains with the incident owner.

Changes to a service require a written scope decision and a revised charge estimate. Small consumables may use the office purchasing process, while managed services require a contract amendment. Procurement closes an action only after receiving the agreed delivery evidence. The next annual review may be brought forward when a material continuity concern remains open.
